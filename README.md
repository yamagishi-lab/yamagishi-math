# yamagishi-math

Machine-checked statements from the papers of Yuuki Yamagishi (山岸勇輝), written in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
The repository covers 79 papers: 13 whose statements are checked as the papers state them, and 66 for which a more general statement is proved here than the paper currently proves (the papers show these with numbers or tables; see the second table). For 19 of the 66 (Papers 36, 141, 202, 205, 208, 340, 373, 391, 412, 488, 579, 674, 696, 935, 945, 954, 1064, 1098 and 1101) only the core of the general statement is formalized; this is marked "core only".

山岸勇輝の論文の定理と法則のうち、数学の中身を Lean 4（Mathlib）で書き、機械に証明を確かめさせたものです。79 本の論文を扱い、13 本は原稿の言明のとおりに、66 本は原稿がいまは数値や表で示している事を、より一般の形の定理として証明しています（二つ目の表。そのうち論文36・141・202・205・208・340・373・391・412・488・579・674・696・935・945・954・1064・1098・1101 の 19 本は芯だけ）。日本語の説明は下にあります。

- Author: Yuuki Yamagishi (ORCID [0009-0006-8587-3450](https://orcid.org/0009-0006-8587-3450))
- Papers: <https://www.yamagishiyuuki.com/yoi/>
- Lean `v4.37.0-rc1`, Mathlib `v4.37.0-rc1` (see `lean-toolchain` and `lake-manifest.json`)
- No `sorry`, no extra axioms, no `native_decide`. The main theorems depend only on the three standard axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).
- The Lean code was written with the assistance of AI (Claude by Anthropic). The statements come from the author's papers (for the second table, the general statements were formulated here from the claims in the papers), and the author is responsible for the content.

## What is not checked

- Lean checks the statements as written in Lean. Whether a Lean statement says the same thing as the sentence in a paper is for a human reader to compare; the correspondence table is there for that.
- Only the mathematical content is formalized (exponent bookkeeping, integrals, "if and only if" statements). The physical readings in the papers, such as "this 4π is a solid angle" or "this 4π is a convention", are outside the formalization.
- For the papers in the second table, the Lean statement is more general than what the paper proves at present. These are generalizations proved here, not the papers' own theorems yet; where an extra condition was needed (for example d ≥ 2 in Paper 156), it is stated in the Lean file and in the correspondence table.
- For 19 papers only the core is formalized (marked "core only"). Not formalized: the Stokes equations themselves and the derivatives of the Stokeslet and dipole (Paper 36; the strain and pressure on the sphere are taken as input), the population-level single crossing for log-concave populations (Paper 579), the bias coefficient of the up-down method (Paper 935), the asymptotic decomposition of the lattice sum (Paper 1098), the Lane–Emden equation itself (Paper 141; its solution constants are inputs), the dimension counts d²(d − 1)/2 and C(d, 3) (Paper 202), the classification of isoperimetric regions on the flat torus (Paper 205), the limit of the shell fraction (Paper 208; a bound valid at every radius is proved instead), the bound for two points on different latitudes (Paper 340), the periodicity of the orbits (Paper 373), the asymptotic formula and the mean of the highest 1/q of the waves (Paper 391), the planarity of the Voronoi adjacency graph (Paper 412; it is assumed), the uniqueness of the isochrone by Abel inversion (Paper 488), isostaticity and the frictional count d + 1 (Paper 674), the limits to 0 and K (Paper 696), the legality of Go positions and the transfer matrices (Paper 945; that legal boards placed side by side stay legal is assumed), the recurrence classification of the up-down chain (Paper 954), the dimension counts and the connection, torsion and curvature part (Paper 1064), and Crofton's formula with the mean chord 4V/S (Paper 1101). In Paper 1040 the sky average is defined as a one-dimensional integral with weight (1 − μ²)^((d−3)/2), and the statements about F(d), F(d + 1) and F(p) = 1 are proved for d ≥ 3.
- Some models and standard formulas are taken as definitions rather than derived, for example the Gaussian curvature K = −f″/f of dr² + f²dφ² (Paper 1027), the Carnot work (Paper 534) and the expected maximum written as an integral (Paper 391). Each Lean file and the correspondence table say what is taken as a definition or an assumption.
- Some sentences in the papers were found to be false as written: Paper 710 says the turning point is set by the difference of the exponents alone (true only when the revenue exponent is 1), and Paper 1040 says the sky average equals 1 only for p = d − 1 (p = 0 also gives 1). In Paper 236 the family of solutions of X² = I misses the matrices with b = 0, and "0 or 2 roots in a finite field" fails in characteristic 2 (one root); in Paper 534, requiring constant work for one-degree steps only does not determine the logarithmic scale (a counterexample is proved); in Paper 696 the decline below the threshold is not fastest at the lowest numbers (N = 10 vs 50); in Paper 960 the exact proportionality of the cheapest whole number of tanks fails (at a = 0.6 and remainder 10^(−30), 61 tanks cost less than 60). Paper 738's monotonicity in q needs ρ ≥ −1/2. Details are in the correspondence table.
- Paper 1123 (silent currents and the shape of the conductor) was examined but not formalized: Green's identities on general domains and the identity theorem for harmonic functions in three dimensions are not yet in Mathlib. Papers 33 (the Wigner angle as an area), 364 (selection raises the mean and shrinks the spread) and 940 (the tailed ladder) were also examined but not formalized: they need, respectively, the decomposition of Lorentz boosts with the area of hyperbolic triangles, Sampford's inequality for the truncated normal distribution, and a comparison of Laplacian eigenvalues, none of which is in Mathlib yet. The reasons are recorded in the correspondence table.
- A machine-checked proof says nothing about novelty. Several of these statements are elementary, and some are known in other forms (for example, the density r^(−2) of Paper 01 is the singular isothermal sphere of astrophysics). The papers themselves say where a statement is elementary or already known.

## How to check it yourself

Install Lean with [elan](https://lean-lang.org/install/), then:

```
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

`lake build` finishes without errors when every theorem in the repository has been checked.
